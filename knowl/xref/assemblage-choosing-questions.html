Questions to ask about a statement.

  1. Is it true? Try some values that satisfy its conditions. If one of them fails, it is a counterexample, and the statement is false (Chapter 2).
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Is it about a finite number of cases? Then you can check each case: a proof by exhaustion (Chapter 4). Some statements about infinitely many numbers split into finitely many cases, such as even and odd.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Is it about every positive integer \(n\) (or every integer from some value on), where the case \(n + 1\) can be built from the case \(n\text{,}\) as in a sum of \(n\) terms or a sequence defined term by term? Try induction (Chapter 6).
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Is it of the form “if A, then B”? Start from A and work towards B: a direct proof (Chapter 5). If A gives you little to work with, prove the contrapositive or argue by contradiction.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Does it say that something is impossible or not so (“irrational”, “there is no …”, “cannot”)? Assume the opposite and look for a contradiction (Chapter 7).
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
These are clues, not rules.
