Exercise 1.

The following statement is true, but the proof by induction below is wrong. Find the error, and write a correct proof.
🔗
Statement: for every odd positive integer \(n\text{,}\) \(f(n) = 7^n + 1\) is divisible by 8.
🔗
Step 1: base case. When \(n = 1\text{,}\) \(f(1) = 7^1 + 1 = 7 + 1 = 8\text{,}\) and 8 is divisible by 8. So the statement holds for \(n = 1\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 2: induction hypothesis. Assume that the statement holds for \(n = k\text{.}\) This means that \(f(k) = 7^k + 1\) is a multiple of 8, so \(7^k + 1 = 8m\text{,}\) where \(m\) is a positive integer.
🔗
Step 3: induction step. We need to show that the statement holds for \(n = k + 1\text{,}\) that is, that \(7^{k+1} + 1 = 8l\text{,}\) where \(l\) is a positive integer. Starting from the left-hand side,
\begin{align*} 7^{k+1} + 1 \amp = 7 \times 7^k + 1\\ \amp = 7 \times 7^k + 7 - 6\\ \amp = 7(7^k + 1) - 6\\ \amp = 7 \times 8m - 6\\ \amp = 8(7m) - 6, \end{align*}
which is not a multiple of 8.
🔗
Hint.
Check the algebra line by line. If every line is correct, ask whether the proof sets out to show the right thing: which numbers is the statement about?
