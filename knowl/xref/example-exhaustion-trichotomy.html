Example 4.3.1 Using trichotomy.

Prove that the square of any real number is never negative.
🔗
Solution.
Let \(a\) be any real number.
🔗
It is always useful to name the variable first, and the set it belongs to: natural numbers, real numbers and so on.
🔗
To prove: \(a \in \R \implies a^2 \ge 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Where you can, translate the statement in words into a statement in symbols.
🔗
By the trichotomy of the real numbers, \(a \gt 0\text{,}\) \(a \lt 0\) or \(a = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Case \(a \gt 0\text{.}\) Multiplying both sides of \(a \gt 0\) by \(a\) gives \(a \times a \gt 0 \times a\text{,}\) so \(a^2 \gt 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Here \(a\) is positive, and multiplying both sides of an inequality by a positive number keeps its direction.
🔗
Case \(a \lt 0\text{.}\) Multiplying both sides of \(a \lt 0\) by \(a\) gives \(a \times a \gt 0 \times a\text{,}\) so \(a^2 \gt 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Here \(a\) is negative, and multiplying both sides of an inequality by a negative number reverses it: \(\lt\) becomes \(\gt\text{.}\)
🔗
Case \(a = 0\text{.}\) Then \(a^2 = 0 \times 0 = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
So in every case \(a^2 \gt 0\) or \(a^2 = 0\text{;}\) that is, \(a^2 \ge 0\) for every real number \(a\text{.}\)
